# africa-east-south: household electricity price per kWh (October 2026)

Group: Eastern Africa (BI DJ ER ET KM KE MG MU MW MZ RE RW SC SO SS TZ UG YT ZM ZW) and Southern Africa (BW LS NA SZ ZA).
Priced: 15 of 25. Gaps: 10 (see the end).

Consumption: 200 kWh/month for World Bank low-income countries (UG, RW), 300 kWh/month for the rest, as the method sets. No national typical-use figure was sourced. In KE, TZ, UG and RW a real median household uses well under
100 kWh and pays a lifeline rate. So the figure below is the price for a household using that much power, not the
average bill.

Sources were fetched from regulator and utility sites directly. The gaps below are
mostly sites that could not be reached (DNS or connection failures: kplc.co.ke, epra.go.ke, escom.mw,
edd.dj, sonelec.km, ore.mg, portal.edm.co.mz, eea.gov.et) or pages with no tariff on them, not countries without a
tariff.

## Per country

**ZA South Africa: 4.39 ZAR/kWh (high).** Which tariff to use: about 40% of households buy directly from Eskom
(mostly Homelight, low-use, rural and township). The rest, including most metro households, buy from their
municipality, which resells Eskom bulk power at a NERSA-approved but municipal-specific tariff. NERSA does not
publish a household average. I chose a large metro's standard residential tariff: City of Cape Town "Domestic"
(properties valued R500k–R1m, prepaid or credit), 2026/27, from 1 Jul 2026, VAT inclusive: 413.79 c/kWh up to
600 kWh plus a Services and Wires charge of R74.77/month (R2.46/day on prepaid).
300 × 4.1379 = 1241.37, plus 74.77, gives R1316.14, which is **R4.387/kWh**. For comparison: Cape Town Lifeline
(property under R500k) is 283.04 c/kWh with no fixed charge. Cape Town Home User is 355.95 c plus R424.30/month
(R4.97/kWh at 300). Johannesburg City Power prepaid is about R3.3–4.4 (press, unverified). Eskom's Homepower
2026/27 energy rate is 355.56 c/kWh excl. VAT plus daily fixed charges (press figure; the Eskom 2026/27 schedule's
tables are images). Eskom direct increase: 8.76% from 1 Apr 2026. Municipal increase: 9.01% from 1 Jul 2026.

**ZW Zimbabwe: 4.49 ZWG/kWh (medium).** The ZERA-approved ZETDC domestic prepaid tariff is set in US dollars and
stepped (the quota resets monthly): 1–50 kWh 8 USc, 51–100 9, 101–200 16, 201–300 23, 301–400 25, over 400 26, plus
a 6% REA levy. It is sold in ZWG at the prevailing interbank rate: 2.2697 / 2.5534 / 4.5394 / 6.5254 / 7.0928 /
7.3765 ZWG incl. REA as of 4 Oct 2026, which implies about 26.8 ZWG/USD.
At 300 kWh: 50×2.2697 + 50×2.5534 + 100×4.5394 + 100×6.5254 = ZWG 1347.64, which is **4.49 ZWG/kWh**
(USD 0.168). The source is a secondary transcription (Zimpricecheck) of the ZERA schedule, because ZERA's and
ZETDC's own sites have no tariff page. Domestic supply is not shown with VAT on token receipts (not verified).

**KE Kenya: 27.49 KES/kWh (low).** The EPRA tariff schedule assigns a household to one category by its average
use: Lifeline ≤30 kWh at 12.23, 31–100 kWh at 16.45, over 100 kWh at 19.08 KES/kWh. The rate applies to all units.
Monthly pass-throughs: fuel energy cost charge KSh 3.00 (Sep 2026, press); forex adjustment KSh 0.7541 (Jun 2026,
the latest I could find); WRA levy 0.0142; EPRA levy 0.03; REP levy 5% of the base charge; VAT 16% on base plus fuel
plus forex. The inflation adjustment is unknown and omitted (it is small).
At 300 kWh: 5724 + 900 + 226.23 + VAT 1096.04 + REP 286.20 + EPRA 9.00 + WRA 4.26 = KSh 8245.73, which is
**27.49 KES/kWh**. EPRA and KPLC were unreachable, so every input comes from press reports of the gazette notices.
Hence low confidence.

**TZ Tanzania: 356 TZS/kWh (medium).** The EWURA fact sheet (Dec 2025) gives T1 (general usage, the
residential default above 75 kWh) at 292 TZS/kWh with a service charge of 0. This has been unchanged since the
1 Apr 2016 tariff order. TANESCO adds VAT 18%, REA levy 3% and EWURA levy 1%, usually shown as 22% on top:
292 × 1.22 = **356.2**. If the levies compound it is 358.3. The levy stacking is from bill practice, not the
regulator's document. D1 (≤75 kWh) households pay 100 TZS/kWh, and 350 for units above 75.

**UG Uganda: 920 UGX/kWh (medium).** ERA's Q3 2026 schedule (Jul–Sep, dated 1 Jul 2026) for UEDCL Code 10.1
Domestic:

- Lifeline: 250 for the first 15 kWh, only where the 6-month average is ≤100 kWh.
- 16–80 kWh: 779.4.
- 81–150 kWh: 412 (Cooking Tariff, registered customers only).
- Over 150 kWh: 779.4.
- No fixed charge.

At 200 kWh (no lifeline, not on the cooking tariff): 779.4, plus 18% VAT, gives **919.7**. ERA's Q4 2026 schedule
is listed (1 Oct 2026) but the download has no file attached. The press release puts the domestic "average" at the
same 779.4 for Q3. VAT on domestic supply is assumed to be standard 18%.

**RW Rwanda: 392 RWF/kWh (medium).** RURA public notice, effective 1 Oct 2025, VAT exclusive. Residential
blocks: 0–20 kWh at 89, 21–50 at 310, over 50 at 369.
200 kWh: 1780 + 9300 + 55350 = 66,430; with 18% VAT that is 78,387, which is **391.9 RWF/kWh**. At a more
typical 40 kWh it is about 214 RWF/kWh with VAT.

**ZM Zambia: 1.50 ZMW/kWh (medium).** ERB Multi-Year Tariff for ZESCO, effective 1 Nov 2025 to 31 Oct 2026.
Residential inclining blocks: R1 0–100 kWh at 0.35, R2 100–200 at 1.00, R3 200–400 at 2.42, R4 over 400 at 3.45.
No fixed charge.
300 kWh: 35 + 100 + 242 = K377, then excise 3% and VAT 16% gives K450.47, which is **1.50 ZMW/kWh**. At 200 kWh it
would be 0.81. From 1 Nov 2026 the pre-approved 2026/27 rates apply: 0.54 / 1.28 / 2.83 / 4.04, about 2.03 ZMW/kWh
at 300 with taxes. The excise and VAT treatment comes from ZESCO token-receipt practice and is not in the schedule.

**MU Mauritius: 6.92 MUR/kWh (medium).** URA General Notice 473 of 2026 sets temporary charges from 1 May 2026,
a rise of up to 15%. Tariffs 110/120/140 use these blocks (Rs/kWh):

- First 25 kWh: 3.63.
- Next 25: 5.04.
- Next 25: 5.45.
- Next 25: 6.27.
- Next 100: 7.07.
- Next 50: 8.07.
- Next 50: 9.09.
- Next 200: 12.03, and so on.

300 kWh: 90.75 + 126 + 136.25 + 156.75 + 707 + 403.5 + 454.5 = Rs 2074.75, which is **6.92 MUR/kWh**. The Tariff
120 minimum charge of Rs 211.60 does not bind. Meter rental is excluded. I treated domestic electricity as carrying
no VAT; that is not verified, hence medium.

**SC Seychelles: 2.02 SCR/kWh (low).** PUC domestic Tariff 120 (2.4–9.6 kVA): 0–200 kWh at 1.83, 201–300 at
2.12, 301–400 at 3.76, 401–600 at 4.45, over 600 at 5.10 SCR/kWh. Demand charge 4.9 SCR per subscribed kVA;
I assumed 6 kVA.
300 kWh: 366 + 212 + 29.4 = SCR 607.4, which is **2.02**. Not included: the monthly fuel-price variation (0.28 cents
per 1 cent/litre change in fuel price) and any VAT. The page gives no effective date.

**RE Réunion: 0.227 EUR/kWh (medium).** The regulated tarif bleu (TRVE) is the same as mainland France because of
perequation. The grid from 1 Aug 2026 is from the EDM spreadsheet: Base 6 kVA, 11.94 EUR/month and 13.8815 c/kWh
excluding taxes. Add the accise of 0.03085 EUR/kWh (rate from EDM's page) and TVA at the DOM rate of 8.5%.
(11.94 + 41.64 + 9.26) × 1.085 = 68.18, which is **0.227 EUR/kWh**. CTA is left out (small). EDF Réunion's site
returned 403, so the taxes were not confirmed on an EDF Réunion page.

**YT Mayotte: 0.214 EUR/kWh (high).** The same TRVE grid from Électricité de Mayotte (1 Aug 2026), plus the
CSPE/accise of 0.03085 EUR/kWh and the internal octroi de mer of 2.5% on subscription plus consumption. TVA is 0%.
11.94 + 41.64 + 9.26 + 1.34 = 64.18, which is **0.214 EUR/kWh**.

**BW Botswana: 1.24 BWP/kWh (high).** BPC tariff from 1 Aug 2026, VAT inclusive. Domestic (TOU 4): fixed charge
P35.12/month; band 1 (≤200 kWh) 0.7507; band 2 (over 200 kWh) 1.5637; levy 0.10/kWh.
300 kWh: 35.12 + 150.14 + 156.37 + 30.00 = P371.63, which is **1.24**.

**NA Namibia: 2.58 NAD/kWh (high).** ECB Schedule of Approved Tariffs for the City of Windhoek, valid from 09/2026.
Residential prepaid: energy 2.4298, ECB levy 0.0212, NEF levy 0.0160, local authority surcharge 0.1100. No capacity
charge on prepaid. Residential supply is zero-rated for VAT. Total **2.577 NAD/kWh**, flat. Postpaid residential is
1.6616 plus N$15.45 per ampere per month (60 A gives about N$927/month), so per kWh it is higher for postpaid. Most
households are prepaid.

**SZ Eswatini: 2.86 SZL/kWh (high).** EEC 2026/27 S1 Domestic is a flat 2.8624 SZL/kWh, including the 2.5% rural
electrification levy, with no facility charge. VAT applies only to non-domestic customers. The Lifeline S10 tariff
is 1.5685 for 0–75 kWh, 2.8624 for 76–100 and 6.2973 above 100. The effective date was taken as the FY start,
1 Apr 2026.

**LS Lesotho: 2.29 LSL/kWh (low).** The LEWA-approved LEC Domestic energy charge for 2025/26 (from 1 Apr 2025) is
M2.0816/kWh, including the customer levy (0.0423) and the rural electrification levy (0.035), excluding VAT. It is
flat, with no fixed charge. I applied 10% VAT, which I believe is Lesotho's rate on electricity but have not verified:
**2.29**. LEWA's site shows no 2026/27 determination, so the 2025/26 tariff may still apply or may have been raised.

## Currency

- **ZW: ZWG (ZiG).** Tariffs are denominated in USD by ZERA but billed and sold in ZWG at the interbank rate. Most
  domestic prepaid meters vend in ZWG, and a USD channel exists. Sources:
  https://zimpricecheck.com/price-updates/zesa-tariffs/ ("Domestic customers are billed in ZiG at the prevailing
  exchange rate") and https://www.zimbabwesituation.com/news/zesa-pegs-electricity-tariffs-in-us-dollars/ (USD
  peg, payable at the interbank rate, Feb 2024). The CSV figure is in ZWG; the USD equivalent is about 0.168.
- **SS South Sudan: SSP** officially. Juba's JEDCO has historically sold prepaid power in SSP (not priced; see Gaps).
- **SO Somalia:** in practice USD. Private utilities (BECO in south-central Somalia) bill in US dollars; SOS is not
  used for utility bills. Not priced.
- **RE, YT: EUR.** **SZ:** SZL (the ZAR also circulates; EEC bills in SZL). **LS:** LSL (at par with ZAR). **NA:**
  NAD (at par with ZAR).
- **DJ:** DJF. **ER:** ERN. **KM:** KMF. **BI:** BIF. None of these were priced.

## Zone candidates

None was confirmed at 2× or more with the data gathered.

- **ZA South Africa** is the closest candidate. Its 9 provinces are well known, but the price split is between
  municipal and Eskom supply, not between provinces. Eskom-direct Homelight 20A households (2026/27 table not
  readable; about R2.3/kWh incl. VAT in 2025/26, from press, unverified) pay roughly half of what a Cape Town Domestic
  household pays (R4.39). Within municipal areas the spread is about 1.3×: Cape Town Lifeline R2.83, Cape Town
  Domestic R4.39, Johannesburg about R3.3–4.4. It is not a clean regional split.
- **MG Madagascar:** JIRAMA has historically set tariffs by tariff zone (by grid or region). These were not sourced
  this round, so the size of the gap is unknown.
- **SO Somalia:** each city has a separate private grid (Mogadishu, Hargeisa, Garowe, …). Prices probably differ by
  city, but not by recognised federal member states. Unsourced.
- **NA Namibia:** the regional distributors differ (Windhoek 2.58; Erongo RED, NORED, CENORED not computed), but
  probably by well under 2×.

## Gaps

- **ET Ethiopia:** EEU's site (eeu.gov.et) has no tariff page, and eea.gov.et did not resolve. The 2024–2028 phased
  residential tariff revision could not be verified.
- **MG Madagascar:** jirama.mg has only a 2020 explainer (tariff 14 vs 16) and no rates; ore.mg was unreachable. A
  2025 tariff revision is reported but not sourced.
- **MW Malawi:** escom.mw was unreachable. MERA's 2023–2027 base tariff determination gives only all-customer
  averages: K123.26/kWh from 1 Sep 2023, then annual tranches of +16%, +12% and +9%, about K175 by Sep 2026 before any
  ATAF and VAT. There is no domestic tariff table.
- **MZ Mozambique:** EDM's tariff pages are JavaScript placeholders or link to a host (portal.edm.co.mz) that does
  not resolve. ARENE's tariff page is empty.
- **BI Burundi:** REGIDESO's /tarifs/ page is empty, and AREEN shows only water tariffs.
- **DJ Djibouti:** edd.dj was unreachable.
- **ER Eritrea:** EEC publishes no tariff online.
- **KM Comoros:** sonelec.km was unreachable.
- **SO Somalia:** no national tariff. BECO's site shows no prices.
- **SS South Sudan:** no regulator or JEDCO tariff online, and the SSP has been highly unstable.

None of these countries lacks a tariff; each gap is a source that could not be reached.
